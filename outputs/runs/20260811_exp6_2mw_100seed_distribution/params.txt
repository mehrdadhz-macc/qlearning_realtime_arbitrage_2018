Experiment 6: 100-seed paired distribution, 2 MW rate (mirrors Experiment 2)
================================================================================

100 independent trials, each reward trained on the SAME 100 seeds
(paired design: trial i's seed is identical for Reward 1 and Reward 2,
so the comparison isn't contaminated by unrelated exploration luck).
Seeds generated from --seed 42 (train.py's seed-generation RNG), single
pass over 2016, at this project's best confirmed single-pass hyperparameters,
at the paper's OTHER battery configuration (2 MW rate instead of 1 MW).

Parameters
----------
data:                  data/train/isone_rt_hourly_lmp_2016.csv
capacity_mwh:          8.0
max_rate_mw:           2.0
n_price_bins (M):      5
price_bin_method:      quantile
bin_calibration_hours: 720
alpha:                 0.5
gamma:                 0.9
epsilon:               0.9
smoothing (eta):       0.001
efficiency_charge:     1.0
efficiency_discharge:  1.0
n_passes:              1
n_trials:              100
seed:                  42  (generates the 100 trial seeds; not used directly)

Results -- average and interval across the 100 seeds (raw online training, epsilon=0.9)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reward 1: mean $2,060.84   std $3,586.78
Reward 2: mean $4,137.40   std $3,427.57
Paired diff (Reward 2 - Reward 1): mean +$2,076.56   paired t-stat: 18.07
Reward 2 wins 99/100 paired trials.

See training_distribution_plot.png for the full per-seed scatter with mean
+/- 1 std, and training_cumulative_profit_over_time_plot.png for the mean
+/- [10th,90th] percentile band over the full 8784-hour training pass.

Held-out (2017) evaluation
----------------------------
Each of the 100 trials' Q-tables was also frozen (epsilon=0, no further
learning) and replayed greedily on data/test/isone_rt_hourly_lmp_2017.csv.
See held_out_2017_eval_plot.png, held_out_2017_eval_summary.json.

Reward 1: mean $11,554.37   std $8,791.37
Reward 2: mean $3,998.89   std $7,683.48
Paired diff (Reward 2 - Reward 1): mean -$7,555.48   paired t-stat: -7.09
Reward 1 wins 75/100 paired trials (Reward 2 wins only 25/100).

Unlike Experiment 2 (1 MW), where the held-out difference was merely not
significant (t=1.26), at 2 MW it REVERSES decisively -- Reward 1
significantly beats Reward 2 on held-out data, despite Reward 2 winning
training 99/100.

Greedy-after-training (2016) evaluation -- isolating learned-policy quality
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Epsilon=0.9 never decays during training (Algorithm 1), so ~90% of every
trial's actions are random exploration -- the raw training number above is
dominated by exploration cost, not a clean read of what the learned
Q-table alone achieved. Each trial's final Q-table was ALSO frozen and
replayed greedily (epsilon=0) on the SAME 2016 data it trained on
(evaluate.py --data data/train/isone_rt_hourly_lmp_2016.csv --label
greedy_2016 --max-rate-mw 2.0). See greedy_2016_eval_plot.png /
greedy_2016_eval_summary.json.

Reward 1: mean $11,284.61   std $7,955.23
Reward 2: mean $3,679.68    std $6,669.03

This flips the training-time story entirely: on the raw online number,
Reward 2 leads (99/100 wins); on greedy-after-training, Reward 1 leads by
a wide margin ($11,284.61 vs $3,679.68), and this greedy ranking matches
the held-out ranking (Reward 1 wins) rather than the raw training ranking
(Reward 2 wins). The raw training-time "Reward 2 wins" result at 2 MW is
almost entirely an exploration-noise artifact, not a reflection of which
reward function actually taught the better policy.
