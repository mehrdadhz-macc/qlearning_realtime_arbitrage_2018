Experiment 2: 100-seed paired distribution, Reward 1 vs Reward 2
====================================================================

100 independent trials, each reward trained on the SAME 100 seeds
(paired design: trial i's seed is identical for Reward 1 and Reward 2,
so the comparison isn't contaminated by unrelated exploration luck).
Seeds generated from --seed 42 (train.py's seed-generation RNG), single
pass over 2016, at this project's best confirmed single-pass hyperparameters.

Parameters
----------
data:                  data/train/isone_rt_hourly_lmp_2016.csv
capacity_mwh:          8.0
max_rate_mw:           1.0
n_price_bins (M):      5
price_bin_method:      quantile
bin_calibration_hours: 720
alpha:                 0.5
gamma:                 0.9
epsilon:               0.9
smoothing (eta):       0.001
efficiency_charge:     1.0
efficiency_discharge:  1.0
n_passes:              1
n_trials:              100
seed:                  42  (generates the 100 trial seeds; not used directly)

Results -- average and interval across the 100 seeds
------------------------------------------------------
Reward 1: mean $1,264.31   std $2,073.25   95% CI +/- $406.36   range [-$2,574.77, $7,227.58]
Reward 2: mean $3,208.36   std $2,173.64   95% CI +/- $426.03   range [-$1,754.88, $8,029.65]

Paired comparison (Reward 2 - Reward 1, same seed each trial)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
mean difference: +$1,944.05   std $825.77   paired t-stat: 23.54
Reward 2 wins 100/100 paired trials.

This is a much cleaner and more statistically decisive result than the
earlier 100-trial comparison run at this project's original, untuned
hyperparameters (M=10, eta=0.1), which found Reward 2 winning only 83/100
trials with paired t=9.97. At these tuned hyperparameters (M=5, eta=0.001),
Reward 2's advantage over Reward 1 during training is not just significant
on average -- it holds for every single one of the 100 tested seeds.

See training_distribution_plot.png for the full per-seed scatter with mean
+/- 1 std, training_distribution_stats.json for the same numbers in
machine-readable form, and training_cumulative_profit_over_time_plot.png
for the mean +/- [10th,90th] percentile band over the full 8784-hour
training pass (all files above are TRAINING-time profit, i.e. accumulated
online as the Q-table learns, same methodology as the paper's own Fig. 4).

Held-out (2017) evaluation
----------------------------
Each of the 100 trials' Q-tables was also frozen (epsilon=0, no further
learning) and replayed greedily on data/test/isone_rt_hourly_lmp_2017.csv,
a year the agent never trained on -- this is an ADDITIONAL, stronger check
beyond what the paper itself does (the paper never holds out a test year).
See held_out_2017_eval_plot.png, held_out_2017_eval_summary.json (raw
per-trial results, produced by evaluate.py), and
held_out_2017_distribution_stats.json (paired stats, matching the format
of training_distribution_stats.json).

Held-out results -- average and interval across the same 100 seeds
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Reward 1: mean $6,590.73   std $5,878.82   range [-$5,736.92, $21,739.65]
Reward 2: mean $7,713.17   std $7,110.87   range [-$7,899.76, $25,388.67]
Paired diff (Reward 2 - Reward 1): mean +$1,122.43   std $8,913.93   paired t-stat: 1.26
Reward 2 wins 53/100 paired trials.

Unlike training, Reward 2's advantage over Reward 1 is NOT statistically
significant on held-out data (|t|=1.26 < 1.98 needed) and barely beats a
coin flip (53/100) -- consistent with this project's earlier finding at
the original untuned hyperparameters (see README "Known findings"):
Reward 2's edge is real and large during training, but doesn't reliably
carry over to an unseen year.

Greedy-after-training (2016) evaluation -- isolating learned-policy quality
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Epsilon=0.9 never decays during training (Algorithm 1), so ~90% of every
trial's actions are random exploration -- the raw training number above is
dominated by exploration cost, not a clean read of what the learned
Q-table alone achieved. Each trial's final Q-table was ALSO frozen and
replayed greedily (epsilon=0) on the SAME 2016 data it trained on
(evaluate.py --data data/train/isone_rt_hourly_lmp_2016.csv --label
greedy_2016). See greedy_2016_eval_plot.png / greedy_2016_eval_summary.json.

Reward 1: mean $5,997.41   std $5,100.81
Reward 2: mean $6,745.48   std $5,969.31

Both means are far above their raw training counterparts ($1,264.31 /
$3,208.36) and much closer to each other -- and to the held-out means
above ($6,590.73 / $7,713.17) -- than either is to its own raw training
number. Greedy-after-training profit (on either 2016 or 2017) is a far
more mutually consistent measure of what was learned than the raw online
training number ever was.
